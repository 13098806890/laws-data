### 最高人民法院经济纠纷案件适用简易程序开庭审理的若干规定

<a id="art-1"></a>最高人民法院  
  
经济纠纷案件适用简易程序  
  
开庭审理的若干规定  
  
（最高人民法院审判委员会第602次会议讨论通过）  
  
法发〔1993〕35号  
  
1、基层人民法院和它派出的法庭收到起诉状经审查立案后，认为事实清楚、权利义务关系明确、争议不大的简单的经济纠纷案件，可以适用简易程序进行审理。  
  
2、原、被告双方同时到庭请求解决纠纷的，可以当即审理，当即调解。  
  
3、原告到庭请求解决纠纷，被告在本地的，可以用书面、电话、请基层组织工作人员捎信等简便方式传唤另一方当事人到庭。被告口头答辩的，记入笔录，可以当即审理；被告要求书面答辩的，可以征求其所需答辩期限的意见，但最长不得超过15天。  
  
经询问双方当事人或者被告答辩后，发现双方争议较大，案情重大、复杂的，转入普通程序进行审理。  
  
4、经双方当事人陈述，权利义务关系明确、事实清楚，在征得双方当事人同意后，可以直接进行调解。调解达成协议，制作调解书发给当事人；即时履行完毕，当事人不要求发给调解书的，可将协议内容记入笔录，不制作调解书。双方当事人对案件事实无争议，只是在责任的承担上达不成协议的，开庭审理时可以在双方当事人对事实予以确认的基础上，直接进行法庭辩论。  
  
5、双方当事人对主要事实陈述不一致，或者庭前调解达不成协议的，可以当即开庭审理，也可以另定日期审理，并告知当事人开庭的时间、地点。  
  
6、开庭前，书记员查明当事人及其他诉讼参与人是否到庭。当事人或其他诉讼参与人没有到庭的，应将情况及时报告审判员，由审判员决定是否需要延期或者中止审理。决定延期或者中止审理的，应及时通知当事人和其他诉讼参与人。原告经传票传唤，无正当理由拒不到庭的，可以按撤诉处理。审判员决定如期审理的，书记员宣布当事人及其诉讼代理人入庭。  
  
7、开庭前书记员先宣布法庭纪律。  
  
8、书记员宣布全体起立，请审判员入庭。  
  
9、书记员向审判员报告当事人及其诉讼代理人的出庭情况，审判员核对当事人及其诉讼代理人的身份，并询问各方当事人对于对方出庭人员有无异议。  
  
10、当事人身份经审判员核对无误，且对对方出庭人员没有异议的，审判员宣布到庭的当事人及其诉讼代理人符合法律规定，可以参加本案诉讼。  
  
11、审判员宣布案由、开庭。  
  
12、审判员宣布审判员、书记员姓名，告知当事人有关的诉讼权利义务，询问各方当事人是否申请回避。  
  
13、原告简要陈述起诉的请求、事实和理由。  
  
14、被告针对原告起诉中陈述的事实提出承认或者否认的答辩。  
  
15、当事人对自己的主张有责任提供证据，各方当事人提供的证据，应经对方辩认、互相质证。  
  
16、证人出庭作证的，应查明证人身份，告知证人作证的义务以及作伪证应负的法律责任。证人作证后应征询双方当事人对证人证言的意见。经法庭许可，当事人及其诉讼代理人可以向证人发问。对确实不能出庭的证人提供的证言，当庭宣读后，也应征询双方当事人意见。  
  
17、当事人对争议的问题可以互相辩论。审判员对当事人在辩论中与本案无关的言辞应当及时制止。  
  
18、经法庭调查、辩论，事实基本清楚后，审判员按原告、被告的顺序询问双方当事人是否愿意调解。调解可以当庭进行，也可以休庭后进行。  
  
19、调解可先由各方当事人提出调解方案。当事人意见有分歧的，要讲明道理、分清责任，促使双方当事人自愿达成协议。审判员也可根据对方当事人的请求提出初步调解方案，征询各方当事人意见。  
  
20、经调解，双方当事人取得一致意见，根据协商的内容起草调解协议，由备方当事人签字或盖章。人民法院应当制作调解书发给当事人。  
  
21、调解达不成协议的，审判员可以当庭宣判。宣判时，审判员与当事人应当起立。宣判内容包括认定的事实、判决的理由、适用的法律依据、判决的结果、诉讼费用的负担、当事人的上诉权利、上诉期间和上诉的法院。  
  
22、书记员宣读庭审笔录或者告知当事人和其他诉讼参与人当庭或者在五日内阅读。  
  
庭审笔录经宣读或阅读，记录无误的，当事人和其他诉讼参与人应当在笔录上签名或盖章；拒绝签名、盖章的，记明情况附卷；认为对自己的陈述记录有遗漏或者差错，申请补正的，允许在笔录后面或另页补正。  
  
庭审笔录，由审判员和书记员签名。  
  
23、审判员宣布闭庭。  
  
24、书记员宣布全体起立，请审判员退庭。  
  
25、审判员退庭后，书记员宣布当事人和旁听人员等退庭。  
  
1993年11月16日

**Article 1** Fa Fa [1993] No. 35  
1. After receiving a complaint, examining it and placing it on file, a Primary People's Court or its dispatched tribunal may apply the summary procedure for trial if it finds the fact is clear, the rights and obligations are definite, and the dispute is not major in a simple economic dispute case.  
2. If both the plaintiff and the defendant appear before the court at the same time to request resolution of the dispute, the court may hear and mediate the case immediately.  
3. If the plaintiff appears before the court to request resolution of the dispute and the defendant is located within the locality, the other party may be summoned by simplified means such as a written notice, a telephone call, or a message sent through a staff member of a grassroots organization. If the defendant makes an oral answer, the answer shall be recorded in the transcript, and the case may be heard immediately; if the defendant requests a written answer, the court may seek the defendant's opinion on the necessary time limit for answer, provided that such time limit shall not exceed 15 days.  
After questioning the parties or after the defendant's answer, if it is found that the dispute between the parties is relatively large and the case is significant or complex, the case shall be transferred to the ordinary procedure for trial.  
4. After the statements of the parties, if the rights and obligations are definite and the facts are clear, the court may directly conduct mediation after obtaining the consent of the parties. If an agreement is reached through mediation, a mediation agreement shall be prepared and served on the parties; if the agreement is performed immediately and the parties do not request a written mediation agreement, the content of the agreement may be recorded in the transcript without preparing a written mediation agreement. If the parties have no dispute over the facts of the case but fail to reach an agreement on the assumption of liability, during the court session the court may directly conduct court debate on the basis of the parties' confirmation of the facts.  
5. If the parties' statements on the main facts are inconsistent, or if pre-trial mediation fails to reach an agreement, the case may be heard immediately in court or on another date, and the time and place of the hearing shall be notified to the parties.  
6. Before the court session, the court clerk shall ascertain whether the parties and other participants in the proceedings are present. If any party or other participant in the proceedings is not present, the clerk shall promptly report the situation to the judge, who shall decide whether an adjournment or suspension of the hearing is necessary. If an adjournment or suspension is decided, the parties and other participants in the proceedings shall be promptly notified. If the plaintiff, having been summoned by a subpoena, fails to appear in court without justifiable reasons, the case may be treated as withdrawal of the action. If the judge decides to proceed with the hearing as scheduled, the clerk shall announce the entry of the parties and their litigation agents into the court.  
7. Before the court session, the clerk shall first announce the rules of the court.  
8. The clerk shall announce that all rise and invite the judge to enter the court.  
9. The clerk shall report to the judge on the presence of the parties and their litigation agents; the judge shall verify the identities of the parties and their litigation agents and inquire of each party whether there is any objection to the persons appearing for the opposing party.  
10. If the identities of the parties are verified and no objection is raised to the persons appearing for the opposing party, the judge shall declare that the parties and their litigation agents present in court meet the legal requirements and may participate in the proceedings of this case.  
11. The judge shall announce the cause of action and declare the court session open.  
12. The judge shall announce the names of the judge and the court clerk, inform the parties of the relevant litigation rights and obligations, and inquire of each party whether they apply for withdrawal.  
13. The plaintiff shall briefly state the claims, facts, and grounds of the action.  
14. The defendant shall present an answer admitting or denying the facts stated in the plaintiff's complaint.  
15. Each party has the burden of providing evidence for its own claims; the evidence provided by each party shall be examined and cross-examined by the opposing party.  
16. If a witness appears in court to testify, the witness's identity shall be ascertained, and the witness shall be informed of the obligation to testify and the legal liability for giving false testimony. After the witness testifies, the opinions of the parties on the witness's testimony shall be sought. With the permission of the court, the parties and their litigation agents may question the witness. For the testimony of a witness who is truly unable to appear in court, after it is read aloud in court, the opinions of the parties shall also be sought.  
17. The parties may debate with each other on the issues in dispute. The judge shall promptly stop any statement made by a party during the debate that is irrelevant to the case.  
18. After the facts are basically clear through court investigation and debate, the judge shall ask the parties, in the order of plaintiff and then defendant, whether they are willing to mediate. Mediation may be conducted during the court session or after its adjournment.  
19. Mediation may begin with each party proposing a mediation plan. If the parties have differing opinions, the court shall explain the reasoning, clarify the responsibility, and facilitate the parties' voluntary agreement. The judge may also propose a preliminary mediation plan based on a party's request and seek the opinions of each party.  
20. If, through mediation, the parties reach a consensus, a mediation agreement shall be drafted based on the content of the negotiation, and each party shall sign or affix its seal thereon. The People's Court shall prepare a written mediation agreement and serve it on the parties.  
21. If mediation fails to reach an agreement, the judge may pronounce the judgment in court. When the judgment is pronounced, the judge and the parties shall stand. The content of the judgment shall include the facts found, the reasons for the judgment, the applicable legal basis, the result of the judgment, the allocation of litigation costs, the parties' right to appeal, the time limit for appeal, and the court to which the appeal may be lodged.  
22. The court clerk shall read the court record or inform the parties and other participants in the proceedings to read it in court or within five days.  
After the court record is read or read by the parties, if the record is correct, the parties and other participants in the proceedings shall sign or affix their seals on the record; if they refuse to sign or affix their seals, this shall be noted in the record and attached to the file; if a party considers that his or her statements are omitted or erroneous in the record and applies for supplementation or correction, permission shall be given to make supplementation or correction at the end of the record or on a separate page.  
The court record shall be signed by the judge and the court clerk.  
23. The judge shall announce the closure of the court session.  
24. The court clerk shall announce that all rise and invite the judge to withdraw from the court.  
25. After the judge withdraws from the court, the court clerk shall announce that the parties and the audience, among others, shall withdraw from the court.  
November 16, 1993
